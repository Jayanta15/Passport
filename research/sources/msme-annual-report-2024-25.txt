1.1 Overview of the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME)

1.1.1 Background:  The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) sector has emerged as 
a highly vibrant and dynamic sector of the Indian economy contributing around 30% of 
India’s GDP, over 45% of India’s exports. It contributes significantly to the economy and 
social development of the country by fostering entrepreneurship and generating large 
employment opportunities at comparatively lower capital cost, next only to agriculture. 
MSME are complementary to large industries as ancillary units. This sector contributes 
significantly to inclusive industrial development of the country. An overview of growth and 
performance of the MSME Sector in the country is provided in Chapter 2.

1.2 Mandate of Ministry of MSME

1.2.1 On 9 May 2007, the erstwhile Ministry of Small Scale Industries and the Ministry of Agro and 
Rural Industries were merged to form the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
(M/o MSME).

1.2.2 The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act was enacted in 2006 
to address different issues affecting MSME, inter alia, the coverage and investment ceiling of 
the sector. The MSMED Act seeks to facilitate the development of these enterprises and also 
enhance their competitiveness. The MSMED Act has the following key provisions:

Ø Establishment of a National Board for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises 
headed by the Minister for MSME, to examine the factors affecting the promotion and 
development of MSME, review the policies and programmes of the Central Government 
and make recommendations in this behalf.

Ø Adoption of a Legal framework for recognition of the concept of “enterprise” which 
comprises both manufacturing and service entities.

Ø It empowers the Central Government to undertake programmes and issue guidelines 
and instructions to develop and enhance the competitiveness of MSME.

Ø Strengthening framework of dispute resolution for delayed payments.

Ø a micro enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or equipment does 
not exceed one crore rupees and turnover does not exceed five crore rupees;

Vide Ministry of MSME’s Notification dated 26th June, 2020, the criteria for classifying 
enterprises were revised, w.e.f. 1st July, 2020, as part of the Aatma Nirbhar Bharat package. 
The revised classification criteria are as follows:-

1.2.3 Definition of Micro, Small and Medium enterprises:

Ø a medium enterprise, where the investment in plant and machinery or equipment 
does not exceed fifty crore rupees and turnover does not exceed two hundred and fifty 
crore rupees.

---

1.2.4 Pursuant to the adoption of the new classification of MSME, Udyam registration portal was 
launched on 1st July, 2020 for registration of MSME. The earlier criterion for classifying 
MSME under the MSMED Act, 2006 was based on investment in plant and machinery/
equipment, with separate thresholds for manufacturing and service units.

1.2.5 The new composite classification for both manufacturing and service units aims to support 
both existing and aspiring entrepreneurs. The distinction between manufacturing and 
service sectors has been eliminated. Additionally, a new criterion based on turnover was 
incorporated alongside the previous investment-based classification. The turnover with 
respect to exports was excluded from the overall turnover for all categories of MSME units, 
enhancing ease of doing business.

1.2.6 Government of India supports and supplements the efforts of the State Governments 
and Union Territory Administration through various initiatives, aimed at encouraging 
entrepreneurship, creating employment and livelihood opportunities, and enhancing the 
competitiveness of MSME in the evolving economic landscape.

## 1.3.12 Grievance Monitoring

The Ministry addresses all grievances through the Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS). In addition to CPGRAMS, the Ministry has launched the SAMADHAAN system, an online grievance monitoring platform specifically designed to track and manage other grievances and suggestions received from MSME. Complementing these mechanisms is the CHAMPIONS portal, which serves as a holistic platform for redressal, guidance, and handholding of MSME, offering support in finance, marketing, technology, and capacity building, while also disseminating information in eleven regional languages via 69 State control rooms nationwide.

## 1.3.12.1 MSME SAMADHAAN: To address issues of Delayed Payment to MSEs

Sections 15-24 of the MSMED Act, 2006 address the issue of delayed payments to Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) by buyers. As per the Act, if payment is delayed beyond 45 days, the supplier (MSE) can approach the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council (MSEFC), which is constituted under the Act in each State/UT.

To provide additional support for addressing delayed payment cases and in line with the objectives of the MSMED Act, 2006, the Ministry of MSME has launched the MSME SAMADHAAN Portal ([http://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/](http://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/)) in October, 2017. This portal offers information on the pending payments of MSEs from CPSEs, Central Ministries, State Governments, and other buyers. It allows MSEs to file their delayed payment complaints online. Once an MSE files a complaint, it is required to be registered by the MSEFC concerned within 15 days.

## 1.3.13 Procurement

## 1.3.13.1 Public Procurement Policy

To ensure an assured market for Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs), the Ministry of MSME notified the Public Procurement Policy for MSEs under the MSMED Act, 2006, effective from 1st April 2012 and mandatory w.e.f. 1st April 2015.

In November, 2018, the Hon'ble Prime Minister launched the "Support and Outreach Programme" for the MSME sector, which included 12 key initiatives for MSME growth. One of these initiatives mandated that Public Sector companies to procure 20% of their total purchases from MSEs, with 4% reserved for MSEs owned by SC/ST.

In line with this, the Public Procurement Policy for MSEs Order, 2012 was amended on 9th November 2018 vide Gazette Notification No. S.O. 5670(E), setting the annual procurement target to 25%, including 4% from SC/ST-owned MSEs and 3% from women-owned MSEs.

1.5 Skill Training Eco-system of Ministry of MSME

Ministry of MSME has developed a robust skilling ecosystem to support the growth of 
micro, small, and medium enterprises by meeting the demands for a skilled workforce in 
both emerging and traditional sectors. To enhance employability and build entrepreneurial 
capacity, the Ministry organizes industry-aligned skill development programs for aspiring 
and existing entrepreneurs, as well as for wage employment.

The Ministry aligns its training courses with the National Skill Qualification Framework 
(NSQF) under the Skill India Mission, reporting to the Ministry of Skill Development and 
Entrepreneurship (MSDE). Trainings are conducted under schemes such as Tool Room and 
Technical Institutions (TRs and ITs), TCSP Assistance to Training Institutions (ATI), National 
SC/ST Hub, Khadi Gramodaya Vikas Yojana, Coir Vikas Yojana, and Mahila Coir Yojana. 
Additionally, demand-driven, customized training courses are offered to address specific 
industry needs.

The amendment provides that “In case of an upward change in terms of investment in plant 
and machinery or equipment or turnover or both, and consequent re-classification, an 
enterprise shall continue to avail of all non-tax benefits of the category (micro or small or 
medium) it was in before the re-classification, for a period of three years from the date of 
such upward change."

## 3.1.3.9 Implementation of PM Vishwakarma Scheme:

The Indian economy's informal sector includes traditional artisans and craftspeople, known as 'Vishwakarmas,' engaged in traditional occupations like blacksmithing, carpentry, and pottery, and so on. To enhance their products, services, and socio-economic status, the Government of India launched the 'PM Vishwakarma' scheme, a fully-funded Central Sector initiative aimed at integrating them into domestic and global value chains.

DFOs have been entrusted with the task of registering the beneficiaries for PM Vishwakarma Scheme as well as taking the scheme to the grassroots level in the country. DFO is also entrusted with the task of coordination with Central Ministries and Departments, State Governments, field level implementing agencies and CGTMSE.

## 3.2 Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC)

The Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC), established under the Khadi and Village Industries Commission Act, 1956, is a statutory body under the Ministry of MSME, engaged in promotion and development of Khadi and Village Industries, with the primary goal of creating employment opportunities in rural areas, in general and urban areas through District Industries Centre (DIC), particularly and bolstering the rural economy. As a key organization in the decentralized sector, KVIC plays a pivotal role in generating sustainable non-farm employment at lower per capita investment. Its activities include skill development, technology transfer, research and development, marketing, and generation of employment/self-employment opportunities in rural regions.

*Khadi Spinning activities Leather Footwear Manufacturing Tool Kits*

## 3.2.1 Main Objectives

The main objectives of KVIC include:-

i. Providing employment opportunities in rural areas, and in urban areas through District Industries Centers (DIC); ii. Producing saleable articles to drive economic growth; and iii. Promoting self-reliance among individuals and building up a strong rural community spirit.
## 3.2.2 Functions

The major functions of KVIC as provided under the KVIC Act, 1956 include: -

i. to plan and organize training of persons employed or desirous of seeking employment in Khadi and Village Industries; ii. to build up directly or through specified agencies reserves of raw materials and implements and supply them or arrange supply of raw materials and implements to persons engaged or likely to be engaged in production of handspun yarn or Khadi or Village Industries at such rates as the Commission may decide; iii. to encourage and assist in the creation of common service facilities for the processing of raw materials or semi-finished goods and otherwise facilitate production and marketing of Khadi or products of Village Industries;
## Annual Report | 2024-25

---

iv. to promote the sale and marketing of Khadi or products of Village Industries or handicrafts and for this purpose forge links with established marketing agencies wherever necessary and feasible;

v. to encourage and promote research in the technology used in Khadi and Village Industries, including the use of non-conventional energy and electric power with a view to increasing productivity, eliminating drudgery and otherwise enhancing their competitive capacity and to arrange for dissemination of salient results obtained from such research; vi. to provide financial assistance directly or through specified agencies to Institutions or persons engaged in the development and operation of Khadi or Village Industries and guide them through supply of designs, prototypes and other technical information, for the purpose of producing goods and services for which there is effective demand in the opinion of the Commission; vii. to ensure genuineness and to set up standards of quality and ensure that products of Khadi and Village Industries do conform to the said standards, including issue of certificates or letters of recognition to the concerned persons.

3.3 COIR BOARD

3.3.1 Introduction

---

## 3.3.2 Objective

India stands as the world's largest producer of coir, contributing over 80% of the global coir fibre production. Employing approximately 7.4 lakh individuals, the majority of whom belong to economically weaker sections, the industry provides livelihood opportunities. Notably, nearly 80% of workers involved in fibre extraction and spinning are women. The coir sector in India is highly diverse, encompassing households, cooperatives, NGOs, manufacturers, and exporters.

The Coir Board plays a pivotal role in promoting the sector's growth and enhancing the living standards of workers engaged in this traditional craft.

## 3.3.3 Functions

Key functions of the Coir Board include:

Ø Promoting exports of coir yarn and coir products and carrying on propaganda for that purpose.

Ø Regulating the production of husks, coir yarn and coir products by registering coir spindles and looms for manufacturing coir products as also manufacturers of coir products, licensing exporters of coir, coir yarn and coir products and taking such other appropriate steps as may be prescribed.

Ø Undertaking, assisting or encouraging scientific, technological and economic research and maintaining and assisting in the maintenance of one or more research institutes.

Ø Advising on all matters relating to the development of the coir industry.

## 3.3.4 Organisation

The Head Quarters of the Board is located at Coir House, M.G.Road, Kochi, Kerala. The Board is running 46 establishments including 25 Showrooms and Sales Depots set up in various parts of India. There are a total number of 231 employees under the Board.

## 3.3.5 Coir Industry in India

Coir is a natural coarse fibre extracted from the husk, the fibrous outer shell of the coconut. Its use dates back to ancient times, with Indian navigators employing coir ropes and cordage as ship cables during voyages to Malaya, Java, China, and the Gulf of Arabia. The industrial production of coir-based matting and floor coverings began in 1859 with the establishment of the first factory in Alappuzha, Kerala.

Originating as a traditional agro-based industry in Kerala, the coir sector has expanded to other coconut-producing states such as Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal, Maharashtra, Assam, and Tripura. With its rich heritage and export-oriented focus, the coir industry holds significant potential for growth through value addition and technological advancements.

Introduction

Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME) serve as the backbone of economic 
development, contributing significantly to employment generation, industrial output, and 
exports. Recognizing their pivotal role, the government has introduced a range of initiatives 
to address the diverse needs of this sector. These initiatives span across critical areas, 
including Credit and Financial Assistance, Skill Development Training, Infrastructure 
Development, Marketing Assistance, Technological and Quality Upgradation, and 
Other Services. Together, these schemes aim to empower MSME by fostering innovation, 
improving competitiveness, and ensuring sustainable growth, thereby strengthening their 
contribution to the nation's economy. A detailed list of the Schemes of Ministry of MSME is 
given below.

4.1 Credit and Financial Assistance

Access to affordable and adequate credit is critical for the growth and sustainability of Micro, 
Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSME). Recognizing this, the government has introduced 
several schemes aimed at providing financial support to MSME, enabling them to meet their 
operational and expansion needs. Below are the major schemes under this theme:

## Free Credit for MSME

Under the CGTMSE Scheme, Guarantees are provided for extending collateral free loans to Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) through banks and financial institutions (including NBFCs). **Key highlights:** Ø The Scheme covers collateral free credit facility (term loan and/or working capital) extended by eligible lending institutions to micro and small enterprises, up to ` 5 crore (w.e.f. 01.04.2023) per borrowing unit. export market by facilitating their participation in international exhibitions and fairs abroad

Central/State Government organizations/Industry Associations to facilitate participation of
