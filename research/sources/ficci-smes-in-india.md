### STUDY CONTEXT

###### Setting the context

Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) have been a crucial part of India's economic landscape for 
many years. Recognizing the importance of SMEs in India's economy, the government has 
implemented various policies and initiatives to support their growth. These include credit programs, 
tax benefits, infrastructure development, technology upgradation schemes, etc.

Despite government support, access to finance remains a significant challenge for SMEs in India. In 
addition, SMEs in India face several challenges, including inadequate infrastructure, complex 
regulatory procedures, skill shortages, and limited market access. Overcoming these challenges is 
crucial for their growth and sustainability.

In the past few years, various factors driving the businesses and society across the world have 
witnessed changes. World is transitioning to a new era of business driven by technology and 
convergence while the challenges of sustaining socio-economic life has become imminent. This has 
brought certain degree of uncertainty and unpredictability in the business environment across the 
globe.

Digitalisation has gained traction and changes in business dynamics brought challenges for industries 
accustomed to old ways. SMEs in India have been increasingly adopting technology and digital 
solutions to enhance their competitiveness. This includes the use of e-commerce platforms, digital 
marketing, cloud computing, and automation tools. Such technological advancements can improve 
productivity and access to markets for SMEs.

Digital tools and platforms have enabled SMEs to expand their reach beyond traditional boundaries. 
They can now access domestic and global markets through e-commerce platforms, social media, and 
online marketplaces. Digitalisation has allowed SMEs to streamline their operations and reduce 
costs. Automation of processes, digital accounting systems, and cloud computing have improved 
efficiency and productivity while minimizing manual labour and administrative tasks. This enables 
SMEs to allocate resources more effectively and focus on core business activities.

Also, impending global challenges such as climate & environmental concerns have come to fore for a 
timely resolution impacting industries in terms of environment sustainability.

SMEs often have limited financial and human resources to allocate towards sustainability initiatives. 
Implementing sustainable practices may require upfront investments, additional training, and 
dedicated personnel, which can be challenging for SMEs. Many SMEs have limited knowledge and 
awareness of sustainability practices and their business benefits. They may lack the necessary 
expertise to develop and implement sustainability strategies, measure their impact, or navigate 
complex regulations related to sustainability. Government support, industry associations, and 
initiatives aimed at promoting sustainability practices among SMEs can play a crucial role in 
overcoming these challenges.

External factors impacting industry also had bearing on the financial viability and regulatory 
compliance of the companies. These dynamic changes have percolated as major headwinds for 
smaller industrial units such as the SMEs.

---

The task at hand is to find pragmatic solutions to the issues and challenges not just at the policy level 
but also at industry level, with a view to strengthen the SME eco-system and make them the key 
pillars of sustained high economic growth.

###### Study objective

The objective of this study was to understand the issues and challenges faced by small and medium 
enterprises (SMEs) focusing on manufacturing sector in India.

The issues and challenges focused on the following key aspects, namely:

• Financing

• Compliance

• Digitalisation

• Sustainability

**Financing Compliance Digitalisation Sustainability** COMPLIANCE

In view of the above, FICCI commissioned a market research study to IPSOS to understand the issues 
and challenges faced by small and medium enterprises (SMEs) in the manufacturing sector in India, 
with a special focus on aspects related to Digitisation and Sustainability.

The research methodology involved a combination of quantitative and qualitative research, 
including face-to-face interviews with SME owners and key decision-makers, as well as focus group 
discussions with industry experts and regional association members. The study covered seven 
sectors and included a total of 610 respondents through face-to-face interviews and three focus 
group discussions from various cities across India (Agra, Rourkela and Chennai).

##### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

In recent years, world economies have been confronted with a series of unprecedented challenges. 
The rapid spread of the Covid-19 pandemic brought profound disruptions across various sectors,
which was then further aggravated due to the geo-political conflicts. The consequences of these 
events have been far-reaching, affecting everything from supply chains to inflation rates, thus
impeding the growth of businesses and the overall economy.

Nevertheless, amidst the turbulence, economic recovery has again started to gather momentum. 
The resilience of businesses has played a significant role in navigating through these uncertain times. 
By adapting to new circumstances, embracing innovation, and using the power of digital 
transformation, organizations have found ways to overcome challenges.

While the path to full recovery may take time, the signs of progress are becoming increasingly 
evident. Governments, international bodies, and businesses alike are working continuously to 
rebuild their economies.  Further, it is crucial for businesses to remain vigilant and adaptive to the 
evolving landscape.

SMEs are currently recognized as the driving force behind India's economic and social development. 
By embracing digital technologies, SMEs have an even greater opportunity to thrive, grow, and 
succeed. The digitisation of SMEs (Small and Medium Enterprises) in India is an ongoing process 
aimed at leveraging digital technologies to enhance the efficiency and competitiveness of these 
businesses. The government has been actively promoting digital transformation of SMEs to drive 
growth, foster innovation, and improve access to markets.

There are several initiatives taken by the government and private sector to support the digitalisation
of SMEs in India. The government has been investing in improving digital infrastructure, including 
internet connectivity and mobile networks, to enable SMEs to access and leverage digital 
technologies. Mobile technology is playing a significant role in the digitalisation efforts of SMEs in 
India. With the increasing availability of smartphones and affordable data plans, SMEs are leveraging 
mobile applications and platforms for various business functions, including communication, 
payments, inventory management, and marketing. The promotion and adoption of digital payment 
systems, such as UPI (Unified Payments Interface) and mobile wallets, have facilitated cashless 
transactions for SMEs, enhancing financial inclusion and convenience.

SMEs have been leveraging e-commerce platforms and online marketplaces to reach a larger 
customer base and expand their market presence. These platforms provide opportunities for SMEs 
to showcase and sell their products and services nationally and globally. SMEs can utilize various 
digital tools and channels, including social media platforms, search engine optimization (SEO), and 
online advertising, to promote their products and services and target specific customer segments. 
Cloud-based solutions offer SMEs flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in managing their 
data, applications, and infrastructure. Cloud computing enables SMEs to access resources ondemand and reduces the need for expensive IT infrastructure. SMEs can leverage digital 
technologies like Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Robotic Process Automation (RPA) to automate 
routine tasks, streamline processes, and improve operational efficiency. The Indian government and 
industry bodies are focusing on imparting digital literacy and skills training to SME owners and 
employees to ensure they can effectively adopt and use digital technologies.

---

The government and other stakeholders are investing in skilling programs to enhance digital literacy 
and technical capabilities among SME owners and employees, enabling them to effectively adopt 
and utilize digital technologies. These measures aim to empower SMEs by enabling them to digitalize 
their operations, enhance competitiveness, and harness the potential of the digital economy.

However, challenges such as connectivity issues, cybersecurity concerns, and lack of digital 
awareness and skills remain, which need to be addressed for wider digital adoption among Indian 
SMEs and contribute to their growth and development.

The study explored the usage of digital technology by SMEs in different business functions, with 60 
percent of enterprises using digital tools for human resources, 51 percent for sales and marketing, 
and 48 percent for finance. Further, the accounting software and business applications like ERP and 
CRM were the most commonly used digital tools by SMEs. It was further confirmed by most of the 
enterprises that they have experienced improvements in productivity and reduction in operational 
costs as a result of incorporating technology into their business and adopting tools such CRM and 
ERP.

In terms of online markets and sales platforms, 71 percent of enterprises had their own channels or 
sales platforms in the form of their own website to either display or sell products. The adoption of 
online markets and sales platform has increased their sales, profits as well as reach to customers. 
However, certain challenges also came to fore including tough competition, lower profit margins and 
lack of skilled manpower to manage such platforms.

Overall, over half of the enterprises rated their ease of compliance on the GST portal as moderate, 
while approximately 43 percent rated it as positive. SMEs also suggested simplifying tax 
compliances, labour and employment laws, accounting standards, import-export regulations, etc.

In terms of sustainability, given the contribution of SMEs to the economic growth, they can play a 
significant role in making a positive impact on the environment and society as a whole. By adopting 
sustainable practices, SMEs can mitigate their ecological footprint, optimize resource utilization, and 
minimize waste generation.

As per our study, an overwhelming 72 percent of respondents were generally aware of sustainability 
and ESG practices, whereas 28 percent were unfamiliar with them. The importance of buyer support 
in implementing sustainability and ESG practices was instrumental as 64 percent of enterprises 
received guidance and support from their buyers.

Over the past two years, a significant number of enterprises have taken steps to adopt sustainable 
practices. The highest proportion of enterprises (41 percent) implemented measures to eliminate 
paper usage, demonstrating a growing awareness of the need to reduce waste and conserve 
resources, conserve water (41 percent), invest in green projects (39 percent), etc. The respondents 
also highlighted their priority for implementing sustainable practices over the next two years, which 
included the usage of renewable energy (36 percent), and investment in green projects (35 percent).

It was further observed that about 64 percent of participating enterprises have realigned their 
businesses as per buyer’s requirements and have thus invested in sustainable practices in response 
to the growing demand for sustainable practices by their buyers.

Although the benefits of implementing sustainable practices are evident, there are several 
challenges that firms may face while implementing them. The respondents have indicated that time constraint (42 percent) is among the key challenges in adopting sustainability practices, followed by 
limited funding (41 percent) and a lack of incentives for implementation (41 percent).

In-addition, enterprises have suggested ways to improve the adoption of sustainability and ESG 
practices by SMEs. Approximately half of the respondents suggested increasing awareness through 
targeted campaigns, training programs, and other awareness-raising initiatives, providing subsidies 
or other benefits to incentivize compliance (35 percent). This could include tax breaks or other 
financial incentives for SMEs that prioritize sustainability and ESG practices.

The study has highlighted the growing significance of digitalisation and the potential benefits it 
offers to SMEs in terms of increased productivity, reduced operational costs, and expanded market 
reach. The study also underscores the importance of sustainability and ESG practices and the role of 
buyer support in their implementation. Additionally, the study report findings also emphasized the 
importance of access to finance for SMEs, and the need for simplified compliance procedures.

These findings can serve as a valuable resource for policymakers, industry associations/experts, and 
SMEs themselves in addressing the challenges and leveraging the opportunities in the manufacturing 
sector.

###### Suggestions on MSME financing

**Change In Classification Norms of MSMEs for NPAs:** The working cycle of MSMEs in many 
cases extends much beyond 90 days period as from the date of purchase of raw materials till 
the date of making sales which spreads over a period of 90 days and thereafter, it takes 
another 30 to 90 days to realize the bills and receivables. Hence, the working capital cycle of 
MSMEs keeps prolonging due to delays in realization of sales receivables. Due to such delays,
which have become a general business practice now, MSMEs find it extremely difficult to 
service their debt instalments or interest obligations in time, as a result of which, many of 
them default and their dues become irregular and overdue, leading to their classification into 
NPAs. There is a need to reconsider the provision of 90 days limit for classifying their over 
dues into NPAs.

It is recommended that the 90 days limit fixed by RBI for classifying overdue of MSMEs should 
be increased to 180 days so that MSMEs are not constrained to divert their working capital 
towards servicing of their loan-instalments and clearing of their overdues at the cost of 
normal business operations. This improvement in the RBI guidelines will save a large number 
of MSMEs from turning sick or getting closed resulting in loss of economic activity and 
employment. This will also prevent avoidable classification of bad debts and unwarranted 
litigation by banks thereby saving the banks from losses.

**Increase emphasis on cash flow-based lending:** Lending to the MSME sector should move to 
cash-flow based lending which replaces lending based on physical asset as collateral, with 
information-based system lending.

The key suggestions to improve the performance of these platforms include:

1. **Automatic Publishing of GST registered MSMEs’ invoices on TReDS and Deemed** 
**Acceptance by Corporate:** It was suggested that the registration at TReDS should be 
mandatory for all companies having the turnover of more than Rs. 250 crore, as it would increase the purview of TReDS (Currently it is Rs 500 crore and above). It is also suggested 
that every tax invoice raised by GST registered MSME unit, should reflect automatically on 
the respective TReDS platform where MSME unit is registered i.e. linking of GST with 
TReDS. After the delivery of Services or Goods, within a certain number of days, the 
corporate must accept or reject the automatically published SMEs’ invoice. After that 
period, the published SME’s tax invoice should be deemed accepted and should be 
available for Financial Institutions to provide fund to SMEs. If the corporate rejects any 
invoice, a mandatory clarification must be sought which should be visible to Banks & 
respective SME units.

2. **Participation of State Government Agencies in TReDs:** SME Enterprises which have 
rendered the services under the Central schemes where implementation is at the State 
Government level are deprived of the benefit of facility of Bill Discounting through this 
platform, given that the State Government agencies are not mandatorily required to 
register on TReDS platform. For greater effectiveness, it should be made mandatory for all 
State Governments’ agencies who are implementing Central Government Funded Schemes 
on the ground, to get enrolled on all or any one of the TReDS (Trade Receivables 
Discounting System) Platform so that SMEs can get their bills discounted from TReDS.  In 
addition, the coverage could be expanded to include all States' PSUs. This will help in 
addressing the cash flow problems of the MSMEs who are working for Central Government 
Funded Schemes in various States.

3. **Make Customer Care Helpline of TReDS more robust:** The existing customer care helpline 
has been reported to be ineffective as there is limited response and tickets generated take 
a long time for resolution of issues. Promptly addressing and resolving the grievances of 
customers would enhance user experience.

4. **High Cost:** SMEs feel that the cost of using the GeM platform, set at 0.5 percent of the 
order value, is high. There should be exemptions or reduced rates to alleviate the financial 
burden on SMEs.

5. **Payment Assurance and Timelines:** In case of GeM, while the payments are assured, the 
time taken for receiving the payments varies. Some mechanism to be made to ensure 
timely payments to SMEs.

Over the last few years, there has been increased focus on digitalisation and many businesses 
have adopted various digital tools in their businesses including digital payments, own 
websites, sales through e-commerce, digitalisation of business, etc. During FGDs, the 
respondents were asked regarding the adoption of digitalisation practices by SMEs.

They have shared that the SMEs in India have embraced digital technology, particularly digital 
payments like credit cards and UPI, making transactions easier and reducing the need for 
physical bank visits. The government's proactive approach to digitalisation is appreciated by 
businesses. However, it was highlighted that there is a need for subsidies for cloud-based ERP

The key digital technologies and related services adopted by SMEs are mentioned below:

1. **Digital payment methods:** SMEs have shifted from traditional methods like cheques to

perceive the presence of OTP mechanism as a safeguard against unauthorized payments.

3. **Digital marketing and taxation:** An equalization levy is currently applicable to payments 
made for, ‘online’ advertisement on platforms of foreign entities. The foreign company 
unwilling to take on the burden of this levy generally passes on to the businesses seeking 
advertisement services. The levy of tax can hugely impact the business of new start-ups or 
small businesses which do not have a significant share in the market needs advertising to reach to the consumers. The levy of such tax increases the cost of doing business by adding 
to the marketing cost. SMEs and Indian start-ups are major users of digital advertising 
platforms.

The key challenges faced by the SMEs that are inhibiting their growth and integration with ecommerce platforms include -

1. **Limited product suitability:** E-commerce platforms are typically designed towards the sale 
of consumer goods, which are often smaller in size and quantity. For SMEs dealing with 
larger and bulk orders, the e-commerce platforms have limitations (especially related to 
raw material).

2. **Challenge in logistics:** E-commerce platforms are primarily designed to handle last-mile 
delivery for smaller packages. However, when dealing with larger and heavier materials, 
the logistics become more complex.

3. **Lack of customization options:** SMEs operating in industries like engineering often offer 
specialized products with unique specifications. E-commerce platforms tend to focus on 
standardized products, making it difficult for these businesses to showcase and sell their 
custom-made items.

**4. One-to-one deal requirements:** In high-value industries, each transaction may involve 
extensive negotiations and customization to meet the specific needs of customers. The 
one-to-one nature of these deals does not fit into the typical model of e-commerce where 
products are often sold in a standardized and automated manner.

---

5. **Limited market reach for B2B materials:** E-commerce platforms primarily focus on 
business-to-consumer (B2C) transactions. Therefore, SMEs dealing with business-tobusiness (B2B) materials may struggle to find the right e-commerce platforms that cater to 
their needs and have customer base in the B2B segment.

#### 3. SUSTAINABILITY

Sustainability and ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) practices are a set of criteria used by 
businesses, investors, and other stakeholders to evaluate a firm’s sustainability and ethical impact. It

society.

- The environmental criteria include carbon footprint, energy efficiency, waste management, 
etc. of businesses.

- Social criteria include labour standards, employee diversity, community engagement, etc.
- Governance criteria include transparency, board diversity, shareholder rights, etc.

In view of ESG's importance, many companies adopt sustainability and ESG practices to report their 
progress to demonstrate their commitment to sustainability and responsible business practices.

Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) in India. Businesses have recognized the importance of 
integrating Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) practices into their operations, not 
only to meet regulatory requirements but also to improve their competitiveness and 
reputation.

Some common sustainable practices being adopted by SMEs include reducing energy and 
resource consumption, using eco-friendly materials, implementing waste management 
programs, and promoting social welfare initiatives such as gender equality and community 
development. SMEs are also increasingly investing in renewable energy solutions such as solar 
power, which not only reduce their carbon footprint but also offer cost savings in the long run.

##### 3.3 Key challenges faced in adopting sustainability practices:

Although the benefits of implementing sustainable practices are evident, there are several 
challenges that firms may face while implementing them. Some of the key challenges include 
lack of resources or funding, limited knowledge and expertise in sustainable practices, 
resistance to change within the organization, and difficulty in measuring the impact of 
sustainability efforts, etc.

According to the survey, majority respondents (42 percent) have indicated that time 
constraint is among the key challenges in adopting sustainability practices, followed by limited 
funding (41 percent) and a lack of incentives for implementation (41 percent). Furthermore, 
more than 3 out of 10 respondents reported a lack of knowledge on how to measure the 
success of implemented ESG projects (34 percent) and how to implement ESG frameworks 
and solutions (31 percent) as other challenges in adopting sustainable practices.

In order to overcome these challenges, enterprises may invest in sustainability training and 
education, define clear goals and metrics for sustainability efforts, and establish partnerships 
and collaborations with other stakeholders in the industry.

##### 3.4 Suggestions to improve adoption of sustainability practices by SMEs:

Approximately half of the respondents suggested increasing awareness about sustainability 
and ESG practices. This can be achieved through targeted campaigns, training programs, and 
other awareness-raising initiatives.

Providing subsidies or other benefits to incentivize compliance was suggested by 35 percent of 
respondents. This could include tax breaks or other financial incentives for SMEs that prioritize 
sustainability and ESG practices.

Other suggestions included providing incentives for compliance and partnering with suppliers 
that follow sustainable practices. This would involve SMEs collaborating with suppliers that 
have established sustainability and ESG practices in order to improve their own sustainability

Suggestions made by respondents include:

1. **Awareness and Education:** There is a need to create awareness and educate SMEs about the 
benefits of ESG practices beyond just being environmentally conscious and demonstrating 
moral responsibility. SMEs must be made aware of the potential cost savings, improved 
efficiency, and access to new markets that can result from adopting sustainable practices. 
Additionally, the misconception that ESG is only for big corporations or foreign companies
needs to be removed.

2. **Incentives and Support:** The government and buyers can provide incentives and support to 
encourage SMEs to adopt sustainable practices. This can include financial incentives, tax 
benefits, access to funding for sustainable initiatives, and technical assistance in 
implementing ESG measures, etc.

3. **Capacity Building:** SMEs often lack the resources and expertise to implement ESG practices 
effectively. Capacity-building programs can be designed to train SMEs on sustainable 
practices, waste reduction, energy efficiency, and other relevant aspects. These programs 
can be organized for SMEs by the government and industry associations.

4. **Market Differentiation:** Educating SMEs for highlighting the value of ESG practices in 
differentiating products and services in the market. By promoting their sustainable 
initiatives, SMEs can attract environmentally and socially conscious customers.

5. **Integration of ESG in Auditing and Reporting:** Government should encourage SMEs to 
integrate ESG considerations into their auditing and reporting processes. This can help them 
track their progress, identify areas for improvement, and demonstrate their commitment to 
sustainability.

6. **Promote Circular Economy Principles:** SMEs can be encouraged to embrace circular 
economy principles, such as reusing, repurposing, and recycling materials, which can reduce 
waste generation and resource consumption.

7. **Facilitate Policy Framework:** Work with policymakers to develop a supportive policy 
framework that promotes sustainability and ESG practices among SMEs. This may include 
setting sustainability targets, providing access to green technologies, and streamlining 
compliance processes for SMEs in India.

#### 4. GROWTH PERFORMANCE OF SMEs AND OUTLOOK

In recent years, global economies have faced significant uncertainties including rapid spread of 
Covid-19, Ukraine crisis, etc. Such unforeseen events have severely disrupted supply chains, led to 
inflationary pressures, and adversely affected the growth of businesses. However, with improved 
situations, now economic recovery has started, and it is gaining pace.

##### 4.1 SMEs Experience during last 2 years:

As per respondents, the experience of SMEs over the last few years has been mixed. While 
government has introduced various schemes and initiatives to support the MSME sector, 
there have been challenges in their implementation and reach. For instance, the Credit 
Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises Scheme (CGTMSE scheme), which 
offers collateral-free loans up to Rs. 5 Crores with government assurance has not been 
effectively accessed by SMEs. The banks have been showing hesitancy in providing financing 
to SMEs. It is suggested that lending to the MSME sector should move to cash-flow based 
lending which replaces lending based on physical asset as collateral, with information-based 
system lending.

The impact of developments over the last few years has varied across industries, with some 
industries being more impacted than others. During the COVID-19 pandemic, government 
provided support through various schemes and measures, which has benefited small and 
medium industries. .

However, there have been some issues faced by SMEs especially related to credit defaults, 
and delayed payments, etc. The need for better coordination between the government, 
banks, and SMEs to address these challenges and provide more effective support was 
highlighted by the respondents.

The respondents have also highlighted the need for improved government policies and 
support mechanisms to address the challenges faced by businesses, particularly in the SME 
sector. They have suggested the need for greater transparency, improving efficiency of 
existing mechanisms and platforms for resolving payment issues, and providing financial relief 
measures that adequately address the needs of businesses impacted by delays and defaults. 
Additionally, there is a need for consideration of the long-term consequences of financial 
difficulties on creditworthiness including negative impact on CIBIL score, long-term 
repercussions and difficulties in accessing future loans.

##### 4.2 Expected performance during next 2 years:

Respondents were asked about the business outlook in next 1-2 years

They highlighted the following challenges that need to be addressed to utilize the potential of 
SMEs.

**Electricity supply:** One of the key challenges faced by industries is electricity supply issues, 
including power tripping. While some improvements have been observed, power tripping 
remains a concern in certain regions.

---

**Land Leasing and Ownership Challenges:** SMEs face hurdles related to land leasing and 
ownership. In the case of industrial lands leased from the government, transfer through 
supplementary deeds is not permitted upon the owner's demise. Moreover, changing 
ownership formats, such as transitioning from proprietorship to partnership, necessitates 
costly re-registration at prevailing market rates and levy of additional stamp duty charges. 
These challenges act as roadblocks to the development of SMEs.

##### 4.3 Suggestions for SMEs growth and enabling them to scale up:

1. **Implementation of Government Schemes:** The government has introduced several 
schemes for SMEs, but the focus should be on effective and timely implementation. 
Proper monitoring and adequate budget allocation are crucial to ensure benefits of these 
schemes to beneficiaries.

2. **Simplified Processes and Self-Certification:** Government approval, processes and 
compliances should be simplified. Self-certification should be considered sufficient for 
certain requirements (non-technical and non-budgetary). This will reduce the 
administrative burden on SMEs and enable them to focus more on their business 
operations.

3. **Digitalisation and Single Window System:** Creating a single window system, where all 
required registrations and certifications can be obtained at the time of starting a business 
will enhance ease of doing business for SMEs.

4. **Capacity Building and Skill Development:** Government should encourage skill 
development and capacity building programs tailored for SMEs. These initiatives will help 
entrepreneurs and employees acquire the necessary knowledge to improve overall 
business efficiency.

5. **Access to Land and Infrastructure:** Since land is a crucial requirement for operating any 
manufacturing business, the government should focus on creating industrial zones and 
parks with easy access to land and necessary infrastructure.

6. **Timely Dispute Resolution Mechanism:** There is a need to strengthen the dispute 
resolution mechanism under the MSMED Act to ensure that SMEs receive timely 
payments and justice in case of disputes with larger companies/ buyers.

7. **Technology Transfer:** Government should establish International Alliances for Technology 
Transfer with various developed and developing countries that give high priority to 
innovation and technology. This institutional mechanism will enable knowledge exchange 
on latest technologies, thereby facilitating innovation driven entrepreneurship in the 
country. The Technology Centres established by Ministry of MSME can play an important 
role in this mechanism.

#### 5. MSMEs CATEGORIZATION

The Government of India has established defined criteria for MSMEs based on investment in 
plant and machinery or equipment and turnover. The definition of MSMEs was recently 
revised by the Government in 2020. As per the new definition, the criteria for defining MSMEs 
in India² are as follows:

**Micro Enterprises:** Enterprises that have an investment of up 
to Rs. 1 crore in plant and machinery or equipment and Rs 5 
crore turnover.

**Small Enterprises:** Enterprises that have an investment of 
up to Rs. 10 crore in plant and machinery or equipment and 
Rs 50 Crore of turnover

**Medium Enterprises:** Enterprises that have an investment 
of up to Rs. 50 crore in plant and machinery or equipment 
and Rs. 250 Crore of turnover.

The MSMEs are eligible for various benefits such as subsidies, tax exemptions, access to credit, 
and other government schemes. The Government of India has been taking various measures 
to promote and support the growth of the MSME sector, recognizing its significant 
contribution to the economy.

##### 5.1 Satisfaction with the defining criteria of MSMEs by Government:

Respondent satisfaction was assessed on the current definition of MSMEs.

63% of the enterprises were satisfied with the current definition of MSMEs and 16% did not 
offer any views; from the 21% who felt a change was required, the primary suggestion was to 
change the current twin criteria of investments in plant & machinery and turnover to the 
criteria of either investment in plant & machinery or turnover. It was suggested that turnover 
could be a better indicator as authorities can readily check the data on turnover through 
platforms such as GSTN. Such ease is not always offered in case of value of investments plus 
one also has to contend with differences between old and new enterprises as the comparison 
will be between historical investments and current investments.

## About FICCI

## Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry

Established in 1927, Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FICCI) is the largest and oldest apex business organisation in India. Its history is closely interwoven with India's struggle for independence, its industrialisation, and its emergence as one of the most rapidly growing global economies.

A non-government, not-for-profit organisation, FICCI is the voice of India's business and industry. From influencing policy to encouraging debate, engaging with policy makers and civil society, FICCI articulates the views and concerns of industry. It serves its members from the Indian private and public corporate sectors and multinational companies, drawing its strength from diverse regional chambers of commerce and industry across states, reaching out to over 2,50,000 companies.

FICCI provides a platform for networking and consensus building within and across sectors and is the first port of call for Indian industry, policy makers and the international business community. FICCI's vision is to be the thought leader for industry, its voice for policy change and its guardian for effective implementation.
