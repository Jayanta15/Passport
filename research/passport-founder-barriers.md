## Key Facts
- **Export readiness is a compliance chain, not just product quality.** DGFT’s *E-Commerce Exports Handbook for MSMEs* (dated 03 Jan 2023; older than 18 months) says exporters need PAN, firm bank account/AD Code, GSTIN and IEC; no export/import may be effected without mentioning IEC. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **Market prioritisation requires evidence beyond ambition.** DGFT advises analysing foreign demand, target segments, shopping behaviour, cultural preferences, seasonality and competitor pricing before choosing a market. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **HS classification is a gating decision.** DGFT says the Indian Tariff Classification (ITC-HS) determines the applicable Indian export policy and helps identify buyer-country requirements; goods can be Free, Restricted or Prohibited, with non-compliance causing Indian penalties or import-country issues. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **Destination rules are product-specific.** DGFT explicitly identifies partner-government-agency certifications (examples: CDSCO for pharma, APEDA for organic exports) and says requirements at the buyer’s country must be confirmed from the classification. This supports why a high-quality product can still fail border clearance. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **Physical fulfilment creates operational and liability exposure.** DGFT requires packaging to withstand repeated handling, climate/temperature/pressure changes, tampering and avoid injury or damage to other parcels. Its postal table lists compensation caps (e.g., EMS up to 130 SDR or value, whichever is less; figures are from 2023 and older than 18 months). URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **Returns are not a simple customer-service event.** DGFT describes consignee/platform-initiated returns, re-import via Courier Bill of Entry, original packing requirements and a Customs Notification 45/2017 duty-exemption reference; it also notes the 2022 courier-return provisions are specific/conditional and do not themselves provide a blanket import-duty exemption. Older than 18 months. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **Tax and documentation add reconciliation work.** DGFT’s process links IGST refunds to accurate shipping-bill data, GSTR-1 Table 6A and mode-specific postal/courier processes; courier shipping requires product info, GST, IEC, HSN and AD code. Rules can change, so current CBIC/GST confirmation is required. URL: https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/rules/cgst_rules/active/chapter10/rule96_v1.00.html
- **Trade costs disproportionately constrain smaller firms.** WTO’s *Trade costs and inclusive growth* (2016; all figures older than 18 months) reports average trade costs equivalent to a 219% tariff in developing countries versus 134% in high-income countries; it identifies customs red tape/inefficient procedures as major components. Using World Bank Enterprise Surveys, it reports that reducing export time benefits SMEs relatively more and could raise SME export shares nearly 20% when delays fall to the regional median. URL: https://www.wto.org/english/res_e/booksp_e/trade-costs-incl-growth_full_e.pdf
- **Indian SME evidence shows administrative friction is material.** FICCI’s *Report on SMEs in India* (September 2023; older than 18 months) reports 34% citing complicated GeM registration, 35% slow payment processing, 43% requesting tax-compliance simplification, and 35% import-export-regulation simplification. It also reports export firms’ compliance/sustainability challenges and complex logistics for larger/heavier products. URL: https://ficci.in/public/storage/SPDocument/23826/gDgHYdVQZUEeikm2MEJ0B8x3z8pqjOjWlYMUyc8d.pdf

## Claims requiring cross-reference
- **Current tax/refund mechanics:** DGFT handbook guidance is 2023 and must be checked against current CBIC/GST circulars, Rule 96/89 and portal practice before operationalising.
- **Destination product rules:** The handbook establishes the need to check them but does not provide country/category-level legal requirements. Validate per SKU and destination with the relevant regulator/importer.
- **Compensation/liability:** Postal compensation caps are not the same as product-liability insurance, consumer-law exposure, marketplace indemnities or carrier terms. Cross-reference destination consumer/product-safety law and contracts.
- **Trade-cost and 20% SME-share estimates:** WTO evidence is cross-country and dated 2016, not an India-specific current estimate. Do not present it as a forecast for an individual Indian brand.
- **FICCI percentages:** Survey findings are industry evidence, not a representative measure of all Indian exporters; confirm sample/methodology and seek newer export-specific surveys.
- **HS classification:** DGFT guidance explains why classification matters, but a real SKU may require customs rulings, broker review or product-specific PGA confirmation.

## Source quality
- **Tier 1 government:** DGFT handbook is a primary Indian government operational guide; useful for process/document requirements, but dated 03 Jan 2023 and partly framed around e-commerce exports.
- **Tier 1 multilateral:** WTO trade-cost study is credible and methodologically useful, but published 2016; figures are historical and cross-country.
- **Major industry evidence:** FICCI September 2023 SME survey provides India-specific operational evidence, but is not a government/multilateral dataset and may not isolate physical-goods exporters.
- CBIC/GST URLs are primary regulatory repositories and should supersede handbook summaries for current tax procedure.
- Search also identified World Bank and ITC resources for cross-checking: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/888791518530457570/pdf/WPS8337.pdf and https://www.intracen.org/resources/publications/export-quality-management-a-guide-for-small-and-medium-sized-exporters-1

## Gaps
- No current, India-specific dataset cleanly quantifying small-brand barriers by SKU, destination and export channel.
- No fetched source gives a complete destination-country matrix for labelling, safety, chemicals, food/cosmetics, packaging/EPR or claims.
- Limited direct evidence on FX volatility, chargebacks, marketplace reserve/holdbacks, landed-cost pricing and insurance for Indian micro-exporters.
- Returns/liability evidence needs destination consumer law, Incoterms, carrier liability and marketplace terms.
- Need current (within 18 months) CBIC/DGFT/ITC/World Bank evidence before using any old percentages in a pilot business case.
