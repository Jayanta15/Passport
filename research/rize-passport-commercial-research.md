# Rize Passport: directional commercial use-case research

**Research date:** Accessed during this research run (verify dates before external circulation)  
**Purpose:** Build a transparent, directional case for a Passport product sold through Razorpay Rize. This is not a forecast and does not use private Razorpay data.

## Key Facts

- **Rize’s own public page describes an early-stage startup program launched in 2021** and lists Tech+, D2C+, and Xport+ communities, including global exports. It reports **1,500+ founders** and **20,000+ company registrations facilitated** (company-reported, not an independently audited merchant count). URL: https://razorpay.com/rize/rize. The page has no clear publication date; freshness is therefore unknown.
- **Razorpay’s public pricing page advertises 2% per successful transaction**, with a 0% platform-fee promotion for the first 90 days, no setup fee/AMC, and custom pricing above ₹5 lakh/month. These are headline terms and can vary by method, contract, taxes, and eligibility. URL: https://razorpay.com/pricing. Page date not shown.
- **DGFT’s MSME e-commerce export handbook (January 2023 document)** says global cross-border e-commerce was estimated at **$800bn by 2025 and up to $2tn by 2030**, and describes FTP 2023 objectives to enable exports by artisans, weavers, craftsmen, and MSMEs. It also describes payment, banking, customs, and logistics frictions. This is an older directional opportunity input (>18 months at the research date), not current India GMV. URL: https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/EcommExportHandbokMSME_E.pdf
- **EY’s July 2024 India report snippet estimates India e-commerce exports at $4–5bn in FY23**, or 0.9%–1.1% of merchandise exports. This is a secondary estimate and older than 18 months at the research date; the PDF should be read and triangulated before using it as a market-size claim. URL: https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/newsroom/2024/07/ey-enabling-e-commerce-exports-from-india.pdf
- **Razorpay’s company blog snippet reports 3% for international card exports and 1% + GST for international bank transfers** (page URL includes 2026). This is promotional pricing evidence, not an independent benchmark; eligibility and date need confirmation in a signed commercial quote. URL: https://razorpay.com/blog/international-payment-processing-cost-in-india-2026-the-complete-rate-benchmark-for-indian-businesses
- **Stripe’s embedded-payments guidance identifies payment markup and subscription-with-payments as monetisation patterns**, while its 2025 vertical-SaaS snippet reports payment attach-rate benchmarks. These are peer/industry patterns, not Razorpay economics. URLs: https://stripe.com/resources/more/revenue-models-for-embedded-payments and https://stripe.com:80/en-ca/lp/vertical-saas-benchmark-2025.

## Transparent scenario

### Definitions

“Eligible merchants” means a planning pool of Rize-linked businesses that meet Passport’s proposed export-product and compliance criteria. **No public source gives this count.** “Activation” means an eligible merchant that completes onboarding and is live for cross-border payments for at least one transaction. “Annual cross-border GMV” is GMV per activated merchant, not total India e-commerce exports.

| Scenario | Eligible merchants (assumption) | Activation (assumption) | Activated merchants | Annual cross-border GMV / activated merchant (assumption) | Passport payment take rate (assumption) | Annual Passport revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 2,000 | 10% | 200 | ₹5 lakh | 0.25% | **₹2.5 lakh** |
| Base | 5,000 | 20% | 1,000 | ₹15 lakh | 0.50% | **₹75 lakh** |
| High | 10,000 | 30% | 3,000 | ₹30 lakh | 0.75% | **₹6.75 crore** |

**Formula:** eligible merchants × activation × annual GMV/activated merchant × Passport take rate.  
Low: 2,000 × 10% × ₹5,00,000 × 0.25% = ₹2,50,000.  
Base: 5,000 × 20% × ₹15,00,000 × 0.50% = ₹75,00,000.  
High: 10,000 × 30% × ₹30,00,000 × 0.75% = ₹6,75,00,000.

These are **illustrative assumptions**, not sourced market facts. The proposed take rate is intentionally below the publicly advertised 2% domestic gateway headline and the search-snippet 3% international-card figure, because Passport could be an incremental routing/compliance/SaaS layer rather than the entire payment processor. It must not be presented as Razorpay pricing. If Passport instead charges a flat fee, the equivalent annual revenue is:

| Scenario | Activated merchants | Illustrative annual Passport fee / merchant (assumption) | Annual fee revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 200 | ₹1,200 | ₹2.4 lakh |
| Base | 1,000 | ₹3,000 | ₹30 lakh |
| High | 3,000 | ₹6,000 | ₹1.8 crore |

The two models should not be added together without deciding whether the fee is bundled or whether payment take rate is incremental. A hybrid pilot could test a small annual fee plus a lower take rate, but no price is sourced here.

## Claims requiring cross-reference

1. **Rize reach:** the 1,500-founder and 20,000-registration figures are Razorpay’s own claims and may not equal active, export-eligible merchants. Obtain a dated export of active Rize businesses and deduplicate registrations.
2. **International pricing:** the 3%/1% figures appear in a Razorpay blog search result; confirm current method-specific pricing, GST, FX/settlement costs, refunds, chargebacks, and eligibility with a commercial quote.
3. **India e-commerce export size:** DGFT’s global forecasts are old and not India GMV; EY’s $4–5bn estimate is a secondary FY23 estimate. Reconcile with current DGFT/RBI/Commerce data and define whether “exports” means shipped merchandise, payment volume, or seller GMV.
4. **Merchant economics:** no public source establishes Passport activation, per-merchant GMV, willingness to pay, or a sustainable take rate. All scenario inputs are assumptions and require a cohort pilot.
5. **Peer monetisation:** Stripe benchmarks and model descriptions demonstrate plausible patterns only; they do not establish a comparable margin or attach rate for Razorpay/Rize.

## Gaps

- No public, audited count of active Rize merchants, D2C merchants, or export-ready merchants.
- No public Passport price, cost-to-serve, fraud/chargeback loss, FX economics, or Razorpay revenue share.
- No current official India e-commerce-export GMV series found in the initial search set.
- No public cohort data for activation, retention, cross-border conversion, average order value, or annual merchant GMV.
- No independent validation that the displayed international-payment rates apply to every eligible Indian merchant or product category.
- Scenario excludes operating costs, GST treatment, refunds, chargebacks, FX gains/losses, support, compliance, and cannibalisation of existing Razorpay payment revenue.
