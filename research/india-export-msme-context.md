# India export and MSME macro context (judge-facing Rize Passport)

**Research cut-off:** 19 September 2026 (the working source snapshots are
dated 19 September 2026; figures are historical and retain the publication
dates below). Currency is US dollars unless noted. “Exports” means
merchandise plus services unless explicitly labelled.

## Key Facts

1. **FY2023-24 all-exports baseline:** India exported **$778.13bn** in
   FY2023-24, up **0.22%** from $776.40bn in FY2022-23. The split was
   **$437.07bn merchandise** (down 3.10%) and **$341.06bn services** (up
   4.84%). The Department of Commerce’s 2024-25 Annual Report says
   April–December 2024 exports were an estimated **$602.64bn**, up 6.03%
   year-on-year; December services were an estimate because the latest RBI
   release then available was November.  
   **URL:** https://www.commerce.gov.in/files/2025-08/Commerce_AR-2024-25-English-1.pdf  
   **Publication:** Department of Commerce Annual Report 2024-25 (February
   2025). *The FY2023-24 figures are now over 18 months old; use as a
   historical baseline, not as a current run rate.*

2. **FY2024-25 full-year result (official preliminary series):** Government
   trade releases report **$820.93bn** total exports, comprising **$437.42bn
   merchandise** and **$383.51bn services**. That implies total growth of
   approximately **5.5%**, merchandise growth of approximately **0.1%**, and
   services growth of approximately **12.4%** versus FY2023-24. Merchandise
   growth therefore materially lagged services.  
   **URL:** https://www.commerce.gov.in/press-releases/  
   **Cross-reference required:** the Commerce press-release index should be
   used to identify the specific release and revision status before putting
   these numbers in a scorecard. The Annual Report snapshot available here
   covers April–December 2024, not the completed FY2024-25. *The FY2024-25
   result is over 18 months old at this research cut-off; label it
   preliminary/revised as appropriate.*

3. **Available FY2025-26 trend signal:** The defensible official comparison
   in the fetched material is **April–December 2024: $602.64bn**, up 6.03%,
   rather than a completed FY2025-26 total. Do not present a FY2025-26
   full-year number until the Commerce Ministry/RBI monthly series has been
   downloaded and reconciled (services are released with a lag and can be
   revised).  
   **URL:** https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/feb/doc202521494001.pdf  
   **Publication:** PIB, “India’s Exports,” February 2025. This is a partial
   period and is over 18 months old; it is not FY2025-26 evidence.

4. **MSME macro scale:** The Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2024-25 describes
   MSMEs as contributing **around 30% of India’s GDP and over 45% of exports**.
   The wording is intentionally approximate and does not define whether the
   export share is an allocation of products by MSME registration, supplier
   linkage, or another estimation method. It is appropriate for context
   (“MSMEs are central to India’s export base”), not for attributing a
   merchant’s export share.  
   **URL:** https://msme.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/cdd1fa9e3553498f59e6fef26bcc34b4.pdf  
   **Publication:** Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2024-25 (2025). The
   underlying national-account and export-share vintages are not fully stated
   in the overview; cross-reference the statistical annex before asserting
   “30% in FY2024-25.”

5. **E-commerce enablement target with a concrete official number:** The
   MSME Ministry’s **MSME Trade Enablement and Marketing (MSME-TEAM)**
   initiative has an outlay of **₹277.35 crore**, runs **2024–2027**, and
   aims to onboard **five lakh MSEs** to e-commerce, including **2.5 lakh
   women-owned MSEs**. Support includes cataloguing, account management,
   logistics and packaging through seller network participants, in
   collaboration with ONDC. As of 31 December 2024, 21 ONDC seller-network
   participants had registered.  
   **URL:** https://msme.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/cdd1fa9e3553498f59e6fef26bcc34b4.pdf  
   This is a policy/enablement target, **not an export-revenue target**.

6. **Export-policy relevance for small sellers:** The 29 July 2025 PIB
   release records DGFT digital reforms, Trade Connect (including a Global
   e-Commerce Guide and “Source from India”), Niryat Bandhu mentoring, and
   reduced application fees for MSMEs using Advance Authorisation/EPCG.
   District Export Hubs and district/state export committees are also cited.
   These support a Passport narrative about discoverability, compliance and
   hand-holding; they do not prove incremental exports or merchant
   conversion.  
   **URL:** https://www.commerce.gov.in/files/d-m-y/PIB2149736.pdf  
   **Publication:** PIB, 29 July 2025. This is within 18 months at the
   research cut-off, but it describes initiatives rather than outcomes.

7. **Sector fit—use carefully:** The MSME Annual Report explicitly lists
   textiles, handlooms, handicrafts, embroidery, leather footwear, toys,
   bamboo/cane crafts and gems/jewellery among products displayed in its
   MSME pavilion. This supports describing these as relevant MSME-facing
   product categories, **not** claiming that Rize Passport users in those
   categories represent national export composition.  
   **URL:** https://msme.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/06/cdd1fa9e3553498f59e6fef26bcc34b4.pdf  
   For a sector export number (textiles/apparel, handicrafts or jewellery),
   obtain the relevant Export Promotion Council/Commerce commodity table;
   the general MSME report is not sufficient.

## Claims requiring cross-reference

- Reconcile the FY2024-25 **$820.93bn / $437.42bn / $383.51bn** series against
  the final Commerce Ministry annual trade release and RBI services data;
  do not mix preliminary monthly estimates with final services data.
- Do not turn “over 45% of exports” into “45% of FY2024-25 exports” without
  locating the Ministry’s underlying year and methodology.
- “E-commerce exports are $4–5bn” appears in an EY July 2024 estimate
  (https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/newsroom/2024/07/ey-enabling-e-commerce-exports-from-india.pdf);
  it is not a government estimate and should not be stated as an official
  total.
- No defensible official national target of “$200–300bn e-commerce exports by
  2030” was established here. Treat that figure as unverified unless a
  primary government source is added.
- Textiles/apparel, handicrafts and jewellery are relevant categories, but no
  category-level export totals should be inferred from their appearance in an
  MSME exhibition list.

## Source quality

- **Tier 1, strongest:** Department of Commerce Annual Report; PIB release
  carrying a ministerial written reply; Ministry of MSME Annual Report.
  These are primary government sources, but estimates/revisions and
  methodology still matter.
- **Tier 1/multilateral cross-checks:** RBI’s balance-of-payments/services
  series and WTO trade statistics should be used to reconcile services and
  international comparability. They are not substitutes for the Indian
  government’s merchandise series.
- **Tier 2:** EY e-commerce estimate is useful market context but is a
  consultancy estimate, not official evidence.
- **Age rule:** FY2023-24 evidence is over 18 months old. FY2024-25 evidence
  is also over 18 months old at this cut-off. The July 2025 PIB policy
  release is not over 18 months old, but it has no measured outcome.

## Gaps

- A fetched, final FY2024-25 Commerce/RBI reconciliation and a current
  FY2025-26 cumulative merchandise-plus-services table are still needed.
- Current FY2025-26 monthly releases should be captured with release dates,
  merchandise values, RBI services values, and revision flags.
- MSME export share needs its source-year/methodology and ideally a
  registration-linked dataset; GDP contribution needs the exact national
  accounts vintage.
- Sector-level 2024-25 exports for textiles/apparel, handicrafts and
  jewellery remain to be sourced from Commerce/EPC commodity tables.
- Official e-commerce export **value** targets and a consistent definition
  (postal/courier/marketplace versus all small-parcel exports) remain
  unresolved.
